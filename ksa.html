Transferable KSA

What you know. What you can do. How you do it.

Knowledge, skills and abilities—commonly abbreviated as KSA—offer a clearer vocabulary for understanding how experience may transfer across industries and occupations.

Knowledge

What a person understands

Examples include customer operations, laboratory practice, regulatory requirements, instructional methods or supply-chain processes.

Across careers: Understanding quality procedures may matter in manufacturing, health services, logistics and regulated technology.

Abilities

Enduring capacities

Examples include oral comprehension, problem sensitivity, deductive reasoning or the ability to recognize patterns.

Across careers: Problem sensitivity can support work in safety, customer escalation, quality assurance and patient services.

Skills

Learned proficiencies

Examples include active listening, project coordination, data analysis, instructing or equipment operation.

Across careers: Active listening can matter in advising, sales, supervision, interviewing and care coordination.

Evidence

How you show it

Examples include work products, outcomes, credentials, supervisor feedback, portfolios, projects and demonstrated practice.

Why it matters: A possible overlap becomes more credible when you can describe where, when and how you used it.

The context matters
